How we deal with possums

Possums are protected wildlife in Victoria, so the aim is to remove them humanely and make sure they can’t get back in.

  1. Inspect the property

    We check the roof space and the outside of the house to find where the possum is getting in, where it is nesting and whether there are young.

  2. Humane cage trapping

    An injury-free cage trap is set in the roof space or near the entry point, so the possum can be caught without harm.

  3. Release on your property

    Under Victorian rules, a trapped possum is released on the same property, within 50 metres of where it was caught, after sunset on the day of capture.

  4. Install one-way doors

    One-way doors are fitted at the entry points so the possum can leave on its own but can’t get back in. We check that there are no young inside before fitting them.

  5. Seal the remaining entry points

    Once the possum is out, we close off other gaps, damaged roof sections and vents, and look at overhanging branches, so it can’t return.

  6. Offer an alternative home

    Where suitable, a possum box can be fitted outside so the possum has somewhere appropriate to live nearby.

Why we don’t relocate possums far away

Moving possums off the property where they were caught is prohibited in Victoria. Possums are very territorial, and relocated possums usually die within days in unfamiliar surroundings. Repellents and ultrasonic devices also do little to deter brushtail possums.

How we deal with rodents

Getting rid of mice and rats is only half the job. The other half is stopping them from coming back.

  1. Inspect and identify

    We look for droppings, gnaw marks, runways and nesting areas to work out whether you have mice or rats, and how they are getting in. Mice can squeeze through gaps about the width of a pencil.

  2. Seal the entry points

    Gaps around pipes, vents, doors and the roofline are closed with rodent-proof materials such as metal mesh, so new rodents can’t get in.

  3. Trapping

    Traps are placed along runways and in areas of activity, and checked and cleared so the problem is dealt with quickly.

  4. Baiting, where suitable

    Where it is appropriate, bait is used in locked, tamper-resistant stations placed away from children, pets and wildlife.

  5. Clean-up and prevention

    We advise on food storage, rubbish and garden clutter that attract rodents, and recommend a follow-up check to make sure they are gone.

Why sealing comes first

Rodents can damage insulation and wiring, and they keep coming back if the entry points stay open. Poison baits can also harm pets and wildlife, which is why trapping and sealing are used wherever possible.
